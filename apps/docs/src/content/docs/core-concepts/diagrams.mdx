## What

The diagram is the state graph drawn from the machine's declared targets.

## Why

A hand-drawn diagram drifts from the code the day someone adds a transition. This one is built from each handler's `to` list, so it is always the machine you run.

The checkout machine's states are `basicInfo`, `shippingAddress`, `payment`, `submitting`, `success` and `error`; its events are `submitBasicInfo`, `submitShipping`, `submitPayment`, `back`, `paymentOk`, `paymentFail` and `paymentTimeout`. Source: ([`checkout.ts`](https://github.com/anderscan/mantaq/blob/main/apps/docs/src/content/docs/checkout.ts)), drawn at build time from each handler's `to` list.

### Read it

A box is a state, an arrow is one target of a `to` list labelled with its event. Every other mark is in the [visualizer guide](/mantaq/guides/visualizer/#nodes).

### Walk it

The panel runs the real machine in your browser. Moving accent arrows are the steps the walk can take next.

1. Click the `submitBasicInfo` arrow: it is sent with the example values. Its `?` opens Details, where you can change them. The walk moves to `shippingAddress`.
2. Click `submitShipping`, then `submitPayment`. Effects do not run here: the arrows out of `submitting` are what `chargeCard` reports.
3. Click `paymentOk`. The walk ends in `success`.
